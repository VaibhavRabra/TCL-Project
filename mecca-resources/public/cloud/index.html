The sovereign cloud and AI checklist for Middle East enterprises

Score each item 0 (not sorted), 1 (partly) or 2 (clear answer). Below 16 out of 24 means the decision needs more work before you sign.

1. Data and residency

  1. Which data must stay in-country? List the systems holding personal, financial, health or government data. National data-protection laws and sector regulators in the region can restrict where this data is stored and processed.
  2. Where is every copy? Include backups, DR copies, logs and analytics extracts, not only the primary database.
  3. Who can access it, and from where? Ask each provider which staff and which countries can reach your data, and how that access is logged.

2. AI and GPU capacity

  1. Training or inference? Training needs bursts of large capacity. Inference needs steady, smaller capacity close to users.
  2. How spiky is demand? If GPUs would sit idle a third of the time or more, owning them is rarely cheaper than renting.
  3. Can the model and its data stay in-region? Check this before a pilot becomes production.

3. Multi-cloud cost

  1. Data transfer. Ask what it costs to move data between clouds and out of each one. It is a common surprise on the bill.
  2. Duplicate services. List tools you now pay for in more than one cloud: security, monitoring, backup.
  3. Commitments. Note minimum terms and what happens if a project pauses or moves.

4. Operations and exit

  1. One view. Can you see performance and spend across all clouds in one place?
  2. Support. Who do you call when a critical workload fails at night or on a weekend, and in which time zone?
  3. Exit. Confirm how you get data out, in what format, and what is deleted afterwards.
